The least-discussed way to improve retrieval: fine-tune **the embedding, not
the model**. On a corpus full of domain jargon this is the cheapest win there is.

<Schema title="a training set out of unlabelled data">
  <Pipeline accent="rose" steps={['Raw chunk', 'Ask an LLM|what question does this answer', '(question, chunk) pair', 'Train the embedding']} />
</Schema>

A general-purpose embedding model has never seen your company's acronyms, part
numbers or internal vocabulary. Synthetic question generation closes that gap
without a single hand-written label.

## Why it works

- The training pairs come from **your corpus**, not from a public benchmark.
- Improving the retriever improves every query in the pipeline — unlike a
  prompt change, it compounds.
- The same synthetic set is reusable as ground truth for retrieval evals.

<Callout type="gotcha">
  Changing the embedding means **rebuilding the whole index**. Old and new
  vectors do not live in the same space. Put that in the cost plan.
</Callout>
